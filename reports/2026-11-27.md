### 1. [Stress-Redistributing Elastomer-Infiltrated Porous OMIEC Networks for Mechanically Robust Organic Electrochemical Transistors](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c15482)

**DOI:** `10.1021/acsami.6c15482`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 87  
**Matched:** bioelectronics, bioelectronic, organic bioelectronics, electrophysiological, organic electrochemical transistor, organic electrochemical transistors, oect, omiec, electrochemical transistor  

**Abstract**

Abstract Stretchable organic electrochemical transistors (OECTs) require mechanically robust organic mixed ionicelectronic conductors (OMIECs) that can maintain stable ion-electron coupling under large mechanical deformation. However, although porous OMIEC architectures are advantageous for enhancing ion transport and deformability, they remain mechanically vulnerable because deformation-induced stress concentrates at pore-interconnecting regions, triggering crack initiation and propagation. Here, we develop an elastomer-infiltrated porous semiconductor (EIPS) strategy to reinforce mechanically fragile OMIEC films through the formation of an interpenetrating semiconductor-elastomer network. Ordered porous OMIEC films are constructed via a hydrophobic-additive-assisted breath-figure assembly, followed by elastomer infiltration to enable efficient mechanical load transfer and stress redistribution throughout the porous framework. Finite-element analysis reveals that elastomer infiltration homogenizes tensile stress distribution, reduces stress concentration at pore walls, and delays crack evolution during deformation. Consequently, the crack-onset strain increases substantially from 14.0 ± 4.2% for bare porous OMIEC films to 44.0 ± 4.2% for EIPS films, enabling EIPS OECTs to remain operational at a maximum tested tensile strain of 80%. Furthermore, the EIPS-based OECT platform demonstrates reliable bioelectronic functionalities, including non-invasive glucose sensing and electrophysiological signal monitoring, under mechanically deformed conditions. These results establish elastomer infiltration as an effective interface-engineering strategy for constructing mechanically resilient OMIEC architectures and advancing robust stretchable organic bioelectronics.

### 24. [Chitosan-Derived Carbon Dots for Fluorescence Detection of Hg2+: Selectivity, Real-Water Assessment, and Computational Insights](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26237505)

**Authors:** Maha G. AlRadadi, Ohoud S. Al-Qurashi, Basma G. Alhogbi, Zoya Zaheer  
**Venue/category:** Sensors  
**Date:** 2026-11-27  
**DOI:** `10.3390/s26237505`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** fluorescent probe  

**Abstract**

Carbon dots (CDs) were prepared from chitosan by a one-step hydrothermal route without dopants or post-synthetic modification and assessed as fluorescent probes for aqueous Hg2+. Characterization revealed mostly disordered carbonaceous nanodomains bearing nitrogen- and oxygen-containing surface groups, with an apparent BET surface area of 223.18 m2 g−1. The intrinsic fluorescence remained stable for 120 h and after six months at 4 °C; the storage stability of the Hg2+ response was not evaluated. Hg2+ caused the strongest quenching among the tested metals (65.22%). Coexisting Ni2+, Cu2+, and Pb2+ attenuated this quenching from 65.72% to 19.53–34.36%, whereas Fe3+ had little effect. The response was linear over 0.5–5 µM (quantification range 0.956–5 µM), and 0.1 µM Hg2+ was clearly detected. Hg2+-induced quenching was retained in well water, wastewater, and tap water, although its magnitude was matrix-dependent. Illustrative density functional theory calculations on neutral-atom carbon quantum dot (CQD) models showed weaker adsorption for Hg@CQD (−0.60 eV) than for Cr@CQD but the smallest HOMO–LUMO gap (0.211 eV); because these models do not represent aqueous Hg2+ and Cr(VI), they cannot establish the quenching mechanism or selectivity. Chitosan-derived CDs are a fast-responding fluorescent platform for Hg2+, although recovery studies and matrix-matched calibration are required for quantitative use.
