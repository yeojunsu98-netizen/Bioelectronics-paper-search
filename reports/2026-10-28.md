### 1. [Real-Time Automated Fluorescence Imaging Assay for Quantifying GFP-Expressing Mycobacterium avium subsp. paratuberculosis](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27219640)

**Authors:** Gerard Badia-Bringué, Arrate Prado-López, Marta Alonso-Hearn  
**Venue/category:** International Journal of Molecular Sciences  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.3390/ijms27219640`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 19  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging, cell imaging, live-cell imaging  

**Abstract**

Measurement of Mycobacterium avium subsp. paratuberculosis (MAP) load within macrophages using a mycobacterial growth inhibition assay (MGIA) is an accurate indicator of host immune control of MAP infection. Real-time fluorescent microscopy allows the continuous analysis of infected cells and the quantification of fluorescence intensity, which directly correlates with concentration of fluorescent bacteria. The objective of this study was to develop an automated and real-time live-cell imaging assay using the IncuCyte SX1® platform for quantifying green fluorescence protein (GFP)-expressing MAP (MAP-GFP) within infected macrophages. The fluorescence of serially diluted free MAP-GFP was quantified using the IncuCyte SX1® demonstrating that the fluorescence signal of 3 × 107 CFU was significantly different from lowest MAP concentrations. Significant differences between uninfected and infected BOMAC cells (MOI; 1:10) in DMEM-F12, DMEM-F12 supplemented with fetal bovine serum (FBS), and RPMI-1640 supplemented with FBS were observed, indicating the suitability of these culture media for measuring MAP-GFP-host cell interaction in the IncuCyte SX1®. For the optimization of the MOI and cell density, bovine monocyte-derived macrophages (MDMs) and the BOMAC cells were seeded at different cell densities and infected with MAP-GFP at different MOIs. After 2 h of infection, the supernatant was removed, the cells were washed three times with HBSS, and fresh RPMI-1640 supplemented with FBS was added to the cells. Then, the fluorescence was quantified in the IncuCyte SX1® at different times p.i. Our results demonstrated that MAP phagocytosis is pathogen number- and cell density-dependent. Overall, the results showed that infection of 1 × 106 cells at an MOI of 1:10 maintained a stable fluorescence signal of approximately 400 to 500 GCU ⋅ μm2/image across 2 h to 4 days p.i. In addition, good correlations between MAP-GFP (logCFUs) estimated in the BACTEC MGIT™ 960 system and fluorescence intensity measured in the IncuCyte SX1® were observed for free MAP-GFP bacilli and from MAP-GFP within infected BOMAC cells, r = 0.79 and r = 0.78, respectively. In conclusion, real-time fluorescent microscopy enables continuous monitoring of MAP-GFP within infected macrophages.

### 2. [Closed‐Loop Bioelectronics: From Functional Materials to Autonomous Therapy](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.75519)

**Authors:** Narlawar Sagar Shrikrishna, Drishya Prakashan, Sonu Gandhi  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Materials  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.1002/adma.75519`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 19  
**Matched:** bioelectronics, bioelectronic  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Closed‐loop bioelectronic systems sense physiological signals, compute adaptive therapeutic decisions, then deliver precisely timed interventions without human oversight, meeting a clinical need that fixed‐schedule treatments cannot address. Achieving this capability at clinical scale requires functional materials that form stable, mechanically matched interfaces with living tissue, sensing and actuation components operating across electrical, chemical, or optical modalities, alongside system architectures that integrate real‐time feedback control with wireless autonomous operation within implantable form factors. This review traces recent advances along the complete closed‐loop pipeline, from functional material families at its foundation, through the transducer technologies and control architectures that enable autonomous operation, to the clinical systems that validate therapeutic outcomes. Spanning neurological, cardiovascular, metabolic, and wound healing applications, closed‐loop operation has advanced from preclinical proof‐of‐concept to human clinical validation, with commercially approved devices now operating across multiple disease domains. Chronic interface stability, the generalization of embedded machine learning beyond controlled experimental settings, and wireless power delivery at clinical tissue depths, alongside the regulatory complexity of next‐generation adaptive combination products, constitute the defining challenges at the current frontier of the field.

### 6. [Intraoperative indocyanine green fluorescence imaging and multimodal deep learning for predicting non-salvage outcomes in pediatric testicular torsion](https://doi.org/10.3389/fped.2026.1936322)

**Authors:** Zheng Fang, Zhongxu Wang, Xingquan Tu, Yun Zhou, Ting Zhang, Mingcui Fu, Mingliu Huang, Lei Zhang, Xu Cao, Xiangming Yan, Shu Dai  
**Venue/category:** Frontiers in Pediatrics  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.3389/fped.2026.1936322`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 16  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging, near-infrared fluorescence  

**Abstract**

Introduction Intraoperative assessment of testicular viability in pediatric torsion remains subjective. We evaluated a multimodal indocyanine green near-infrared fluorescence (ICG-NIRF) deep learning model for predicting a composite non-salvage outcome. Methods This single-center retrospective analysis used prospectively collected images from consecutive boys with surgically confirmed torsion who underwent emergency exploration and ICG-NIRF imaging. Image collection began in March 2022, and model development was undertaken in January 2025. The endpoint was orchiectomy or ultrasound-confirmed atrophy after orchiopexy, defined as an affected-to-contralateral testicular volume ratio &lt;50% at 6 months. Patients were divided into development ( n = 107) and internal evaluation ( n = 46) cohorts. A traditional logistic model was compared descriptively with a two-stream model using operative photographs and standardized 30-s fluorescence images. Results Among 153 patients, 52 (34.0%) reached the endpoint. In internal evaluation, the traditional model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.889 and a Brier score of 0.182. The multimodal model achieved an area under the curve of 0.946 and a Brier score of 0.128. Conclusion Multimodal ICG-NIRF deep learning may provide an objective adjunct for intraoperative decision-making in pediatric testicular torsion. External validation is required.

### 7. [Node‐Encoded Control of Lanthanide Self‐Assembly Enables Predictable and Water‐Stable Polyhedra for Bioimaging](https://doi.org/10.1002/agt2.70469)

**Authors:** Xin‐Ran Gan, Guanning Huang, Shao‐Jun Hu, Ze‐Hong Chen, Fan Yin, Li‐Peng Zhou, Xi Meng, Xiao‐Qing Guo, Tianfeng Chen, Qing‐Fu Sun  
**Venue/category:** Aggregate  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.1002/agt2.70469`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 12  
**Matched:** bioimaging  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Multinuclear lanthanide‐organic polyhedra offer attractive molecular scaffolds for biomedical imaging, while their predictable construction remains challenging because the high coordination numbers and kinetic lability of lanthanide ions often compromise both topological fidelity and aqueous stability. Here, we develop a coordination‐node‐encoding strategy in which a deprotonation‐stabilized terpyridine‐bis(tetrazolate) chelate preorganizes the local lanthanide coordination environment and thereby programs subsequent self‐assembly. Systematic variation of ligand connectivity affords a family of anionic lanthanide‐organic polyhedra spanning triangular, macrocyclic, tetrahedral, and trigonal‐prismatic topologies. Structural analyses and control experiments demonstrate that cooperative pentadentate chelation coupled with deprotonation imposes the directional coordination constraints required for topology‐selective assembly, while the anionic tetrazolate‐rich surfaces enhance the hydrophilic character and aqueous compatibility of the assemblies. The Gd III truncated‐tetrahedral polyhedron exhibits a per‐Gd III longitudinal relaxivity of 27.9 mM −1 ·s −1 at 9.4 T and 298 K, approximately sevenfold higher than that of Gd‐DTPA measured under identical conditions, corresponding to a molecular relaxivity of 167.1 mM −1 ·s −1 . The Eu III analogues display characteristic red luminescence with high photoluminescence quantum yields (up to 54%) and enable low‐toxicity cellular imaging. Mixed Eu/Gd polyhedra further integrate Eu III luminescence and Gd III magnetic‐resonance contrast within a single molecular architecture, enabling luminescence and MR imaging. This work establishes coordination‐node encoding as a modular design principle for predictable lanthanide polyhedron formation and provides a versatile platform for developing multifunctional lanthanide‐based imaging systems with enhanced aqueous compatibility.

### 13. [Wireless Electrical Stimulation via a Conductive Hydrogel Scaffold Synergizes With Stem Cell Transplantation Therapy to Promote Traumatic Brain Injury Repair](https://doi.org/10.1002/adhm.71915)

**Authors:** Jiawei Li, Jingqi Li, Zhanchi Zhu, Zhaoming Song, Zhaojun Wang, Yun Xie, Ying Hao, Wanchun You, Guosheng Cheng  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Healthcare Materials  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.1002/adhm.71915`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** conductive hydrogel  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Electrical stimulation (ES) offers a robust approach to boost the efficacy of stem cell transplantation therapy for the treatment of neurological disorders. Notably, wireless technology blends convenience with the elimination of infection risk posed by wired connections, but the challenge lies in the integration of wireless‐enabled stimulation materials and stem cell carriers. Here, we describe a flexible annular conductive dopamine‐modified hyaluronic acid (HAD)/polyvinyl alcohol (PVA)/carbon nanotube‌ (CNT) hydrogel that acts as a secondary coil to realize wireless ES via electromagnetic induction, while also serving as a three‐dimensional (3D) scaffold carrier for endogenous neural stem cells (NSCs). Established on the annular HAD/PVA/CNT hydrogel with high conductivity and good biocompatibility, wireless ES significantly enhances neuronal differentiation of NSCs. Furthermore, transplantation of the NSC‐laden annular hydrogel into a traumatic brain injury (TBI) rat model modulates the behaviors of both exogenous and endogenous neural cells. The combined therapy of wireless ES and NSC transplantation promotes neuronal differentiation in both the injury zone and hippocampus in association with the PI3K‐Akt pathway, reduces post‐traumatic inflammatory responses, and effectively restores motor and memory functions in TBI rats. This study offers a promising therapeutic strategy of integrating wireless ES with stem cell transplantation therapy for brain injury repair.

### 14. [From Hydrogels to Cosmetic Contact Media: Honeycomb-Structured Reusable Textile Electrodes for Wearable TENS](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c17119)

**Authors:** Katarzyna Wójkowska, Martyna A. Kmita, Sandra Lepak, Weronika Sosnowicz, Paulina Trzaskowska, Aleksandra Kądziela, Małgorzata Jakubowska, Daniel Janczak  
**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsami.6c17119`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** hydrogel electrode  

**Abstract**

Abstract Textile-integrated electrodes are promising for wearable transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS), but their practical use is limited by high dry skin-electrode impedance, mechanical instability, and insufficient durability after washing and deformation. Here, we report reusable screen-printed textile electrodes based on thermoplastic polyurethane/carbon composites and a stretchable honeycomb architecture for low-impedance skin-interfacing TENS applications. Three carbon skin-contact layers: graphite (G), carbon black (CB), and graphite/carbon black hybrid (G:CB) were integrated with a silver current-delivery layer and transferred onto an elastic polyamide/elastane textile. The honeycomb design significantly improved electromechanical stability, reducing strain-induced resistance changes by approximately one order of magnitude under tensile loading. Under 1000 cycles of 20% strain, CB-based electrodes showed the highest stability, with a final relative resistance increase of 52.1% and an absolute resistance below 0.5 Ω. After 10 washing cycles, resistance increased but remained partially recoverable by post-treatment heat pressing, indicating reconfiguration of the conductive network within the thermoplastic matrix. All carbon-based layers were noncytotoxic toward L929 fibroblasts in an indirect MTT assay. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and contact profilometry revealed filler-dependent surface morphologies, with surface roughness decreasing from CB and reaching the lowest values for G. In vivo skin-electrode impedance measurements showed that dry interfaces were strongly influenced by surface contact conditions, whereas hydrogel, glycerin-based cream, and oil-in-water (O/W) emulsion substantially reduced impedance across 0.1–200 Hz. Importantly, low impedance was retained after washing and cyclic deformation when a contact medium was used. These results highlight a synergistic design strategy combining material composition, stretchable architecture, and interface engineering to enable robust, reusable textile electrodes for wearable electrotherapy. Notably, the demonstrated compatibility with simple, nonspecialized contact media suggests that common cosmetic formulations can effectively serve as impedance-reducing interfaces, opening a practical pathway toward accessible, user-friendly TENS systems without reliance on dedicated hydrogel electrodes.

### 15. [Algebraic multicomponent fluorescence lifetime estimation from harmonic phase measurements under two-photon excitation](https://doi.org/10.1088/2050-6120/aeb34e)

**Authors:** Kenji Saijo, Yu Masuda, Maciej Kretkowski, Wataru Inami, Yoshimasa Kawata  
**Venue/category:** Methods and Applications in Fluorescence  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.1088/2050-6120/aeb34e`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** flim  

**Abstract**

Abstract We formulate and numerically evaluate a direct, non-iterative algebraic estimator for multicomponent fluorescence lifetimes using harmonic phase measurements in a two-photon excitation scheme. The method operates on the complex harmonic responses available at the repetition frequency of a femtosecond pulse train and its harmonics. Rather than performing iterative nonlinear lifetime fitting, we map the measured harmonic responses through a linear coefficient-reconstruction step to the denominator polynomial of a rational representation of the multiexponential decay and obtain the decay constants from its roots. This procedure requires neither initial lifetime estimates nor iterative nonlinear minimization. Numerical simulations were performed for a three-component system, with photon-limited performance evaluated using Poisson counting statistics. For the closely spaced lifetime set of 0.5, 1.5, and 2.5 ns, the mean relative lifetime error decreased from 14.49% at a peak expected count of 100 photons per time bin to 5.94% at 1000 photons and 1.31% at 10 000 photons, with each condition evaluated by averaging 500 statistically independent acquisitions. In a matched 500-trial Monte Carlo comparison at 1 × 10 7 expected photons per pixel, the success probabilities were 0.484 for the unconstrained algebraic estimator, 0.854 for the DC-constrained algebraic estimator, and 0.898 for single-start nonlinear least-squares (NLLS). Thus, imposing the DC constraint substantially improved the photon-noise robustness of the algebraic reconstruction relative to the minimally determined unconstrained formulation, while single-start NLLS remained slightly more robust than the DC-constrained algebraic estimator. In a separate matched 500-sample computational benchmark at 1 × 10 8 expected photons per pixel, the median parameter-estimation times were 0.138, 0.095, and 5.552 ms per pixel for the unconstrained algebraic, DC-constrained algebraic, and single-start NLLS estimators, respectively. Relative to the DC-constrained algebraic median, the unconstrained algebraic and single-start NLLS calculations required approximately 1.45 and 58.35 times longer processing time, respectively. These results identify both a constraint-induced robustness improvement within the algebraic formulation and a robustness–computation trade-off relative to iterative fitting. The present work should therefore be regarded as a theoretical and numerical algebraic implementation within the established multifrequency frequency-domain framework. Experimental validation and harmonic-dependent instrument-response calibration remain necessary for practical two-photon FLIM implementation.
