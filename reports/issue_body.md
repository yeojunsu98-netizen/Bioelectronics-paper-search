### 1. [Dynamically adaptive soft bioelectronics for neuro-immunomodulatory cancer therapy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79002-w)

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 48  
**Matched:** bioelectronics, bioelectronic, soft bioelectronics, neural interface, conductive hydrogel, vagus nerve  

**Abstract**

Abstract Many solid tumors remain refractory to immunotherapy due to profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments. Although vagus nerve stimulation offers a direct neural axis to reprogram immune function, its potential in cancer immunotherapy remains unrealized due to the absence of stable, system-level neuromodulation platforms. Herein, we develop a miniature subdiaphragmatic vagus nerve stimulator (SVNS) for near-organ neuromodulation of pancreatic tumor immunity. To construct stable electrical coupling with subdiaphragmatic vagus nerve (SVN) along the esophagus, a dynamically adaptive neural interface comprising an injectable anti-fatigue adhesive conductive hydrogel and a deformable liquid metal electrode is explored. The SVNS is integrated with a battery-free lead-free wireless power module, which can be configured into a Swiss-roll architecture, enabling minimally invasive implantation. In an orthotopic mouse model of pancreatic tumor, chronic SVN stimulation using SVNS suppressed tumor growth and metastasis, reshaped tumor innervation, promoted a shift toward an M1-like TAM phenotype, enhanced cytotoxic immune cell infiltration, and further potentiated the efficacy of anti-PD-1 immunotherapy.

### 3. [Ion-Regulated Hydration Enables Spontaneous Formation of Asymmetric Polymer Networks for Wearable Electronics](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biomac.6c01900)

**Authors:** Qiang Zhang, Xueqing Wang, Qiqi Zhong, Zihan Wang, Fei Zhang, Xuesheng Wang, Ziyi Li, Zejun Wang  
**Venue/category:** Biomacromolecules  
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acs.biomac.6c01900`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 20  
**Matched:** bioelectronics, bioelectronic, wearable bioelectronics  

**Abstract**

Abstract Spatial asymmetry enables polymers to exhibit distinct structures and functions across regions; however, current strategies for constructing asymmetric polymers often rely on multistep processing or complex material designs. Here, we report a salting-out strategy in which ion-specific hydration effects induce spontaneous redistribution of water and polymers during gelation, forming a bilayer polymer network. The gel comprises a hydration-rich, loosely packed conductive sensing layer and a dehydration-dominant, densely packed low-adhesion layer, yielding an approximately fourfold adhesion contrast and over one order of magnitude difference in conductivity. The gel maintains stable mechanical performance over 2400 loading-unloading cycles and enables reliable strain sensing across a wide strain range of 5−300%, supporting monitoring of both large-scale body motions and subtle physiological activities, including joint movement, swallowing, and vocalization. These findings demonstrate that ion-regulated phase separation can serve as a versatile route for programming structural and functional asymmetry in polymers for wearable bioelectronics.

### 7. [Preliminary study on targeting microbubbles with dual-modality ultrasound/near-infrared fluorescence for identifying early inflammatory response in coronary microcirculation dysfunction](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehag785.4741)

**Abstract**

Abstract Background Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) has a high prevalence and is associated with adverse clinical outcomes, highlighting the critical importance of early detection. Current research primarily focuses on the microthrombotic/fibrotic stages of CMD. However, the microthrombotic stage already represents a relatively late pathophysiological alteration in CMD. How to achieve precise diagnosis of CMD at an earlier, inflammatory response stage remains a pressing scientific question. Purpose To establish a novel molecular imaging strategy capable of accurately identifying the early inflammatory response stage of CMD, thereby enabling its precise diagnosis. Methods Based on our preliminary findings identifying Ninjurin1 protein (Ninj1) as a factor upregulated during the early inflammatory stage of CMD, we developed dual-modal targeted microbubbles for detecting this stage by conjugating Ninj1-targeting components with the near-infrared fluorescent material IR780. The general physicochemical properties, particle size, and surface potential of the microbubbles were evaluated. Their stability, targeting capability toward inflamed/damaged endothelial cells in vitro, and contrast-enhanced imaging performance were assessed. Cytotoxicity was also verified. Finally, leveraging the properties of Ninj1-IR780-NBs (abbreviated as T-IR780-NBs) to enhance near-infrared fluorescence and ultrasound imaging signals in vitro, we further applied them in a CMD animal model for validation of dual-modal diagnosis. Conclusion This study designed and synthesized dual-modal targeted microbubbles (T-IR780-NBs) as an imaging probe for application in diagnosing the early inflammatory stage of CMD. The probe demonstrates favorable biosafety and holds significant potential for early clinical diagnosis of CMD.

### 8. [NHC-Modulated Fe (II) Complex for TADF-Assisted Multiphoton Lifetime Imaging: Concurrent Apoptosis Monitoring and Quantification](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11188982/v1)

**Authors:** Qiong Zhang, Yaqin Li, Xiaowei Gong, Yaxiong Wei, Zhou Lu, Xuegang Chen, Hongping Zhou  
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**DOI:** `10.21203/rs.3.rs-11188982/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 13  
**Matched:** bioimaging, thermally activated delayed fluorescence  

**Abstract**

Abstract The application of luminescent iron complexes in bioimaging is severely constrained by ultrafast excited-state deactivation and cellular background interference. Herein, we present [Fe(imp)2]2+, the first Fe (II) complex that enables the emergence of both three-photon excited fluorescence (3PEF) and thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF), accessed via stepwise substitution of terpyridine with N‑heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands to circumvent the intrinsic drawbacks of traditional Fe‑based materials. Mechanistically, the enhanced ligand field reshapes the relative energetics of the metal-to-ligand charge transfer (MLCT) and metal-centered (MC) state manifolds, disfavoring MC-mediated nonradiative decay and opening a thermally activated emissive pathway. More significantly, [Fe(imp)2]2+ serves as an apoptosis-related spatiotemporal cascade-targeting agent, sequentially targeting the plasma membrane, mitochondria, and the entire cell. The TADF-assisted lifetime-resolved signatures render [Fe(imp)2]2+ capable of distinguishing apoptosis from mixed cell death, with a global lifetime evolution spanning 1.1-2.4 ns and a pronounced lifetime heterogeneity of 0.43 ns. Co-incubation of [Fe(imp)2]2+ with anti-apoptotic agents demonstrated that effective cytoprotection is achieved when the probe lifetime dropped below 1.2 ns. This work pioneers NHC-regulated Fe (II) complexes TADF-mediated lifetime probes, offering a new paradigm for transition-metal bioimaging.

**Abstract**

Abstract This study describes the synthesis, photophysical characterization, and bioimaging properties of a series of chalcones derived from apocynin, functionalized with electroactive groups such as phenyldimethylamine (1), triphenylamine (2), tetraphenylethylene (3), and pyrene (4). These molecules featured donor−π–acceptor–donor (D−π–A–D) type architectures designed to promote intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) processes and notable emissive properties. Compounds 1 and 2 exhibited a marked aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) behavior in THF/water mixtures while compounds 3 and 4 showed a marked enhancement of emission upon aggregation (AIE), associated with the restriction of intramolecular motions in the aggregated state. DFT/TD-DFT calculations and electrochemical studies revealed good electronic communications between the electroactive units. Cellular bioimaging studies performed on compounds 1–4 showed that only compounds 1, 2, and 4 are good fluorescent agents exhibiting notable cellular permeability in HEp-2 carcinoma cells. Overall, these findings demonstrate that apocynin α,β-unsaturated systems constitute versatile luminescent platforms with tunable photophysical properties, suitable for applications in cellular bioimaging and the development of remarkable fluorescent materials.

### 11. [Oxidized Pectin-Amine-Modified Chitosan/PEDOT with Decellularized Human Spinal Cord-Based Self-Healing and Electroconductive Hydrogel for Potential Spinal Regeneration Applications](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsbiomaterials.6c00957)

**Abstract**

Abstract Decellularized extracellular matrix (dECM)-based biomaterials have emerged as promising therapeutic platforms for spinal cord injury (SCI) repair due to their ability to provide regenerative biochemical cues. In this study, we developed an injectable, electroconductive, and self-healing hydrogel for potential application in SCI repair. Injectability enables minimally invasive delivery, electroconductivity facilitates electrical communication that may promote neural regeneration, and self-healing properties allow restoration of the hydrogel network following shear stress during injection. The self-healing hydrogel was formulated using Schiff-base chemistry between aldehyde-functionalized pectin (PEC-ALD) and amine-functionalized chitosan (CS). Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) (PEDOT) derivatives were incorporated to enhance electrical conductivity and support neuronal differentiation and axonal outgrowth. In addition, decellularized human spinal cord (dSC), prepared using a modified Hudson protocol, was incorporated to provide tissue-specific pro-regenerative cues. The resulting PEC-ALD-CS-PEDOT-dSC hydrogels exhibited dynamic self-healing behavior characteristic of native spinal cord tissue. Under high oscillatory strain (1000%), the hydrogel network structure temporarily dissociated, while recovery of the crosslinked network occurred upon reduction to low strain (1%). The hydrogels demonstrated a storage modulus of approximately 600 Pa, consistent with the mechanical properties favorable for neuronal growth. Incorporation of PEDOT derivatives resulted in an electrical conductivity of approximately 5.6 mS/cm. Schiff base-mediated crosslinking enabled rapid gelation within 20 min at 37 °C. Furthermore, the hydrogels exhibited shear-thinning behavior, supporting their suitability for injectable delivery. Biological evaluation demonstrated immunomodulatory potential, as RAW 264.7 macrophages encapsulated within the hydrogel exhibited greater CD206 expression relative to CD86 expression, even under lipopolysaccharide-induced inflammatory conditions. Additionally, the hydrogels supported proliferation of human SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells, indicating cytocompatibility and potential to support neural regeneration. Overall, this study presents a multifunctional self-healing and electroconductive hydrogel platform incorporating decellularized spinal cord matrix for potential minimally invasive treatment of SCI.

### 13. [A novel, multifunctional pyrrolopyrimidine-modified chitosan hydrogel for potential applications with enhanced electrical characteristics and antimicrobial activity](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3e91d15efb283a5f2ac428e9db9b6eae50882779)

**Abstract**

Abstract A novel pyrrolopyrimidine-modified chitosan hydrogel ( 3 ) is synthesized and charcterized. Pyrrolopyrimidine compound ( 2 ) is firstly synthesized and then it is used to chemically modify chitosan through the formation of imine linkage. The synthesized compounds are characteized by Fourier transforms infra red spectroscopy (FTIR), proton and carbon nuclear magnetic resonance ( 1 HNMR and 13 CNMR), and Field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM). The prepared hydrogel undergoes multifunctional studies including electrical properties and antimicrobial activity. This research examines the dielectric and alternating-current conductivity characteristics of the pyrrolopyrimidine-modified chitosan hydrogel across different frequencies and temperatures, compared with the pure chitosan sample. Dielectric and AC conductivity properties of pyrrolopyrimidine-modified chitosan hydrogel are systematically investigated across a frequency range and temperatures from 20–90 °C, benchmarked against pure chitosan networks. Pure chitosan exhibits high low-frequency dielectric loss and permittivity due to pronounced Maxwell–Wagner–Sillars interfacial polarization from mobile ions and trapped charges along polar groups, alongside frequency-dependent AC conductivity governed by ion hopping. Pyrrolopyrimidine modification significantly suppresses dielectric loss while preserving permittivity, introduces a characteristic conductivity minimum at intermediate frequencies indicating restricted long-range ion migration, and yields a thermally stable response with reduced dissipative pathways. These enhancements stem from heteroaromatic cross-linking, π–π interactions, and constrained segmental mobility, which limit charge accumulation and leakage. The tailored dielectric and conduction behavior positions pyrrolopyrimidine-chitosan hydrogels as promising materials for electroactive devices, sensors, and energy storage systems requiring balanced polarization and minimal losses. The synthesized compounds are investigated for their antimicrobial activity. They are examined against two gram-positive bacteria ( Bacillus cereus 33,018 and Bacillus subtilis 6633), two gram-negative bacteria ( Pseudomonas aeruginosa 9027 and Salmonella typhimurium 14,028), and two yeast-like fungi ( Candida albicans 10,231 and Candida tropicalis 750). The antimicrobial activity of the target compounds ( 1–3 ) is compared with Amoxicillin for bacteria and clotrimazole for yeast and fungi. The minimum inhibitory concentration for the prepared compounds is detected. The results show that the pyrrolopyrimidine compound ( 2 ) has high activity towards both bacterial and fungal strains. The antimicrobial activity of pure chitosan shows low activity. By modification of citosan by pyrropyrimidine compound, the activity increases towards bacterial strains but no activity towards fungal strains. Based on the results, the synthesized pyrrolopyrimidine derivatives and the modified chitosan hydrogel exhibit useful characteristics for biomedical applications, including potential use as antimicrobial wound-care and drug-delivery materials. Furthermore, the results obtained for the dielectric and AC conductivity properties of the modified hydrogel indicate its potential for use in selected sensing, bioelectronic, and electroactive systems where control of dielectric characteristics, minimal dielectric loss, and antimicrobial properties are required.
