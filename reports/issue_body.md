### 1. [LCD-Loaded Ag2S Nanoparticles for NIR-II Fluorescence Imaging and Precision Therapy of Allergic Rhinitis](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsanm.6c04727)

**Authors:** Yaohua Chen, Huifang Hao, Xiaochun Zheng, Haoyu Liu, Ziliang Zheng, Weiwei Zhang  
**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Nano Materials  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsanm.6c04727`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 29  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging, nir-ii, second near-infrared  

**Abstract**

Abstract Allergic rhinitis (AR) is an allergic inflammatory condition driven by immunoglobulin E (IgE) mediated type I hypersensitivity. Upon re-exposure to allergen, mast cells release histamine, which activates the histamine H1 receptor (H1R), leading to cytokine release, recruitment of inflammatory cells, and accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This cascade amplifies local inflammation and oxidative stress. Levocetirizine dihydrochloride (LCD), a second-generation antihistamine, is commonly used due to its high selectivity for H1R and low systemic toxicity. However, effective visualization of inflamed nasal lesions and targeted delivery of antihistamines to pathological nasal tissues remain challenging. To overcome these challenges, we designed an imaging-guided theranostic nano-delivery system (AL NPs) based on second near-infrared (NIR-II, 1000−1700 nm) fluorescence imaging. Silver sulfide nanoparticles (A NPs) were synthesized through biomineralization to produce stable NIR-II emission, enabling precise imaging and localization of inflamed areas in the nasal cavity. LCD was loaded onto the nanoparticles to counteract H1R-mediated acute reactions, reducing histamine-induced increases in vascular permeability, mucosal swelling, and local oxidative stress. This approach enables the rapid and preferential accumulation of LCD-loaded AL NPs in inflamed nasal tissues following intravenous administration, thereby facilitating NIR-II fluorescence-guided lesion visualization and localized therapy for AR.

### 4. [Host-sensitized lanthanide emission in Egyptian blue for shortwave-infrared bioimaging](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15012807/v1)

**Authors:** Robert Nißler, Heinrich Jaeger, Eleftheria Charalambous, Quanyu Zhou, Ana Maria Torres Caldas, Mattia Moretti, Simone de Brot, Alexander Gogos, Xosé Luís Deán-Ben, Anxo Vidal Figueroa, Daniel Razansky, Grigorios Itskos et al.  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**DOI:** `10.26434/chemrxiv.15012807/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 20  
**Matched:** bioimaging, nir-ii  

**Abstract**

Lanthanide ions emit sharp, photostable shortwave-infrared light but absorb weakly, so efficient sensitization remains a key challenge. Here we show that Cu2+, commonly a quencher of lanthanide luminescence, acts as a broadband, long-lived host sensitizer for Er3+ and Yb3+ in Egyptian blue (CaCuSi4O10) and related copper tetrasilicates (MCuSi4O10, M = Ca, Sr, Ba). Flame-spray-synthesized nanopigments reach quantum yields of 43% (Yb3+) and above 70% (Er3+), including 57% in the NIR-IIb with an exceptionally large Stokes shift of ~900 nm. Spectroscopy reveals Cu2+ centers adjacent to Er3+ that transfer almost completely from the relaxed 2B2g state, whereas distant centers transfer slowly, followed by near-unity Er3+ emission. Transfer efficiency decreases with increasing M-cation size (Ca ≫ Sr > Ba), providing a simple design rule. Because Er3+ remains excited for ~12 ms, transfer saturates at low photon flux, making these pigments most efficient under weak light such as ordinary room illumination. We demonstrate their potential in transcranial super-resolution angiography of mouse cerebral microcirculation, luminescence thermometry (2% K-1) and biocompatibility, establishing hostsensitized copper tetrasilicates as robust, earth-abundant shortwave-infrared emitters for bioimaging.

### 5. [Bayesian Optimization-Assisted Synthesis of High Quantum Yield Boron-Doped Carbon Quantum Dots for Glutathione Selective Determination via Potassium Dichromate-Mediated Fluorescence “Off–On” Switch and Cell Imaging](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules31234317)

**Authors:** Jianhao Xin, Shuqing Jiang, Rong Liu, Jianli Wu, Liyan Li, Lina Fu, Jianhua Xing, Xin Lv, Rong Pang  
**Venue/category:** Molecules  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**DOI:** `10.3390/molecules31234317`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 20  
**Matched:** bioimaging, fluorescence imaging, cell imaging  

**Abstract**

Boron-doped carbon dots (B-CDs) with high quantum yield (QY) were rationally synthesized via a hydrothermal method assisted by Bayesian optimization with Gaussian process regression. Sobol sensitivity analysis quantitatively revealed the dominant precursor factors and their interactive effects on QY, providing mechanistic understanding of boron doping. Under the optimized precursor mass ratio, the as-prepared B-CDs achieved a QY of 51.84%. The material was characterized by UV–Vis spectroscopy, FTIR, XRD, XPS, TEM, Raman spectroscopy and Zeta potential. Investigation of the quenching process of CrO-B-CDs revealed that the quenching behavior is attributed to static quenching mainly induced by oxidation of surface hydroxyl groups, demonstrating that abundant surface hydroxyl groups are the key fluorescence source of B-CDs. Benefiting from this surface-quenching effect triggered by potassium dichromate, a CrO-B-CD “off–on” fluorescent nanoprobe was fabricated for glutathione detection. The sensor exhibited a linear detection range of 0–16.0 μM (R2 = 0.997) and a limit of detection as low as 24.0 nM at pH 6.0 with a reaction time of 2 min. Satisfactory recoveries were obtained for GSH quantification in human serum samples. Moreover, B-CDs displayed favorable biocompatibility and excellent photostability, enabling effective fluorescence imaging in HeLa cells. This study offers a data-driven strategy for fabricating high-performance carbon dots and presents a promising nanosensor for biosensing and bioimaging.

### 23. [Visualization of polarity in tumoral ferroptosis, disulfidptosis and inflammatory arthritis models with a NIR lipid droplet fluorescent probe.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/893e74e2248c71b118ecb73fd3d4ee811489ace5)

**Keyword score:** 14  
**Matched:** fluorescent probe, in vivo imaging  

**Abstract**

BACKGROUND Abnormal polarity changes in lipid droplet (LD) is closely related to the occurrence and progression of many pathophysiological processes. Unfortunately, owing to the lack of appropriate tools and the research on multiple disease models, the visualization of abnormal LD polarity in tumoral ferroptosis, disulfidptosis, and inflammatory arthritis (IA) model mice has not been achieved. RESULTS Herein, we have facilely synthesized a robust near-infrared (NIR) fluorescent probe (LD-P) based on dicyanobenzopyranyl-thiophene-dimethylamino skeleton, which exhibited a typical D-π-A molecular configuration. Upon the polarity level decreasing from 0.3200 to 0.1221, LD-P displayed an approximately 2000-fold fluorescence enhancement with λem > 710 nm, which is beneficial for high-quality in vivo imaging. LD-P can specifically accumulate within the LDs, permitting sensitively monitoring LD polarity changes in living cells. Facilitated by LD-P, we revealed an increased polarity in tumoral ferroptosis, disulfidptosis and λ-carrageenan-induced IA mice models, as well as a decrease in polarity level after administration of therapeutic drugs for the first time. SIGNIFICANCE This work not only provides a new analysis method for monitoring polarity changes, but also elucidates the intrinsic relationship between LD polarity and ferroptosis/disulfidptosis and IA, which can help to promote related pathological mechanisms and personalized diagnosis and treatment research.

### 24. [Design Principles and Recent Advances of Organic Multiphoton‐Active TADF Materials: From Two‐Photon to Three‐Photon Excitation](https://doi.org/10.1002/cptc.70336)

**Authors:** Youhei Chitose, Mageswari Gomathi Vinayakam, Tzu‐Chau Lin, Ja‐Hon Lin, Chihaya Adachi  
**Venue/category:** ChemPhotoChem  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**DOI:** `10.1002/cptc.70336`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 13  
**Matched:** bioimaging, thermally activated delayed fluorescence  

**Abstract**

Multiphoton‐active thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials have emerged as a distinctive class of organic emitters that integrate nonlinear excitation with efficient triplet harvesting. This review highlights recent progress in TADF systems exhibiting two‐photon absorption (2PA) and the emergence of three‐photon absorption (3PA), collectively termed multiphoton absorption (MPA). Highly emissive MPA‐TADF materials capable of generating delayed fluorescence under near‐infrared (NIR) excitation have expanded applications to bioimaging and photodynamic therapy (PDT). Although such systems require sophisticated molecular design, precise control of the balance between locally excited (LE) and charge‐transfer (CT) states has proven crucial for enhancing MPA cross‐sections, reverse intersystem crossing (RISC), and radiative decay processes. These design strategies have also been translated into optoelectronic applications, leading to improved organic light‐emitting diode (OLED) performance. Furthermore, the realization of intrinsic 3PA in purely organic TADF emitters represents a significant advance in the field, enabling NIR‐II excitation while maintaining high photoluminescence quantum yields (PLQYs) and efficient TADF behavior. Collectively, these advances position MPA‐TADF materials as versatile platforms bridging photonics, biomedical applications, and optoelectronics. We conclude by outlining key challenges and future directions for MPA‐TADF materials.
