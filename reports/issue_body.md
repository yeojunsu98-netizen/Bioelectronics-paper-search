**Authors:** Mingyang Yuan

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 57  
**Matched:** bioelectronics, bioelectronic, biointerface, electrophysiological, organic electrochemical transistor, organic electrochemical transistors, conductive hydrogel, electrochemical transistor  

**Abstract**

Flexible bioelectronic devices provide mechanically compliant interfaces for acquiring, processing, and modulating physiological information across skin, organ, and neural systems. Their development has moved from single-parameter wearable sensors toward integrated platforms that combine electrophysiological, mechanical, thermal, and biochemical measurements with wireless communication, soft power modules, and algorithm-assisted interpretation. This review summarizes recent progress in the material basis, interface construction, multimodal sensing mechanisms, and system-level integration of flexible bioelectronics. Emphasis is placed on conductive hydrogels, elastomeric composites, liquid metals, laser-induced graphene, organic electrochemical transistors, porous soft structures, and adhesive interfaces that support low-impedance coupling, strain tolerance, and long-term operation. The review further discusses device designs for electrocardiography, electromyography, electroencephalography, pressure, strain, temperature, sweat metabolites, electrolytes, and inflammatory biomarkers, followed by integration strategies for crosstalk control, energy supply, wireless data transfer, intelligent analysis, and closed-loop feedback. It is demonstrated by current evidence that clinically oriented flexible bioelectronics require coordinated optimization of biointerface mechanics, signal fidelity, biochemical selectivity, manufacturing reproducibility, and data reliability. Future research is anticipated to focus on standardized validation, durable encapsulation, patient-specific algorithms, and therapeutic systems capable of linking continuous sensing with adaptive intervention.

### 2. [The Weight Is in the Reading: Hidden States in Memristors, Memlumors, and Electrochemical Transistors](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12706v1)

**Authors:** Gonzalo Rivera-Sierra, Heyi Zhang, Daiki Nishioka, Takashi Tsuchiya, Juan Bisquert  
**Venue/category:** physics.app-ph  
**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**arXiv:** `2611.12706`  
**Source(s):** arXiv  
**Keyword score:** 28  
**Matched:** organic electrochemical transistor, oect, electrochemical transistor  

**Abstract**

Physical synapses are routinely described by a single number, the weight, that is identified with a conductance, a current, or a luminescence yield. This identification conflates two different objects: the internal state that the device stores, and the signal obtained when that state is read. Building on Chua's formulation of memristive systems, we separate the state variable, which evolves with the excitation history, from the observation map that converts it into a measured signal, and we define the weight as the outcome of a specified read protocol. Three platforms show the consequences: a memristor, in which the same voltage writes and reads; a memlumor, in which light both modifies the state and produces the photoluminescence that reports it; and an organic electrochemical transistor (OECT), in which the gate writes and the channel reads. In the transistor, two states prepared by different histories give the same channel current at a specific drain bias and become distinguishable again when the bias is changed, while the stored charge persists and the gate current continues to separate them. Published experiments provide precedents: a drain-bias-dependent cancellation of the OECT transient, and trapped charges in perovskite memlumors that are invisible to conventional time-resolved photoluminescence but revealed by a read-write-read sequence. A synaptic weight is therefore not a property of the material alone but of the material together with its readout, and reports of synaptic behavior should specify what is stored, what is measured, and how one is inferred from the other.

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 12  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging, fluorescent probe  

**Abstract**

Abstract Fluorescence-guided surgery (FGS) is an emerging approach for real-time tumor visualization and surgical margin assessment, yet its application to prostate cancer remains limited by the scarcity of clinically relevant molecular targets and on-demand targeted imaging agents. Most molecularly targeted imaging strategies for prostate cancer have focused on prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA), underscoring the need for complementary targets that could enable fluorescence imaging across molecularly distinct disease subtypes. Here, we developed and systematically evaluated three far-red fluorescent probes targeting prostatic acid phosphatase (ACP3) by conjugating distinct fluorophores to a small-molecule ACP3 affinity ligand. We characterized the probes across biochemical, physicochemical, cellular, and tissue-based assays. Although all three probes retained nanomolar ACP3 inhibitor potency, ACP3i-SulfoCy5 exhibited the most favorable overall imaging profile, combining high aqueous solubility, low nonspecific retention, and superior tumor-to-background contrast in cellular and ex vivo tissue assays. In a PC3-ACP3 prostate tumor model, topical application of ACP3i-SulfoCy5 generated high-contrast fluorescence that clearly delineated tumor margins. Collectively, these findings establish ACP3 as a promising complementary target for prostate cancer fluorescence imaging and support further investigation of ACP3i-SulfoCy5 for topical fluorescence-guided imaging during prostate cancer surgery.

### 15. [Quercetin-Fe3+ Coordination Mediates Rapid Ambient Gelation of a Composite Hydrogel for Multifunctional Biointerfaces](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c03818)

**Authors:** Leqian Wei, Ruolan Deng, Shasha Wang, Mengqi Shan, Fengkai Zhou, Qian Zhang, Fujun Wang, Lu Wang, Jifu Mao  
**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Polymer Materials  
**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsapm.6c03818`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** biointerface  

**Abstract**

Abstract Rapid preparation of composite hydrogels typically relies on external energy input, forcing a compromise among fabrication simplicity, mechanical durability, and biological functionality. Herein, a redox-active quercetin-Fe3+ (QCT-Fe3+) coordination complex is designed to address this conflict, acting simultaneously as a mild catalyst and an active functional component. QCT-Fe3+ mediated activation of ammonium persulfate enables acrylic acid polymerization in ∼5 s without ultraviolet light or external heating. After optimizing the QCT:Fe3+ ratio and the Fe3+ concentration, chitosan was incorporated to form a poly(acrylic acid)/chitosan composite hydrogel, which gelated in 21.0 ± 1.6 s at room temperature. This hydrogel exhibited a tensile modulus of 103.09 ± 4.98 kPa and an equilibrium swelling ratio of 193.91 ± 11.60% and retained ∼88.7% of its peak stress after 500 consecutive loading–unloading cycles. Multiple interfacial interactions render firm adhesion with wet tissues (63.11 ± 4.00 kPa on porcine skin and 19.31 ± 2.40 kPa on porcine subcutaneous fat). The strain-dependent electrical response, with a gauge factor of up to 2.54, enables conformal monitoring of finger, elbow, and knee movements. Sustained QCT-Fe3+ complex release over 168 h endows the hydrogel with radical-scavenging ability and antibacterial activity. The hydrogel suppresses fibroblasts proliferation and migration selectively while remaining compatible with myoblasts, suggesting potential for wound status monitoring and antifibrotic application. By coupling rapid network formation with mechanical, sensing, and biological functions through a single metal-polyphenol coordination motif, this study provides a versatile strategy for engineering multifunctional hydrogel biointerfaces.

ABSTRACT Modern light microscopy methods, which have provided revolutionary improvements in our ability to visualize molecular mechanisms in human cells and tissues, are used routinely in academic, industrial, and healthcare laboratories. We developed a 6-week introductory course, aimed at students in the third or fourth year of undergraduate biomedical studies with no or minimal microscopy experience, to help them better understand and utilize these techniques. Through a combination of lecture-based learning and highly interactive hands-on workshops, students are first shown the principles that underlie a particular imaging-based assay and then given the opportunity to perform the assay themselves. According to student feedback, this is appreciated as a highly interactive course that puts theory into practice right away, providing knowledge and skills that can be applied outside of the classroom.

### 19. [Minimally Invasive Surgery and Emerging Technologies in Emergency Abdominal Surgery: A Narrative Review Focusing on Indocyanine Green Fluorescence, Robotic Assistance and Artificial Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202611.0948.v1)

**Authors:** Mihaela Misca, Iulian Brezean, Giulia Montori, Mauro Podda, Luca Ansaloni, Alberto Arezzo, Andrea Balla, Carlo Bergamini, Chiara Gerardi, Alessio Giordano, Wanda Luisa Rita Petz, Micaela Piccoli et al.  
**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**DOI:** `10.20944/preprints202611.0948.v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging  

**Abstract**

Minimally invasive surgery (MIS) is progressively transforming emergency abdominal care through high-definition visualization, indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence imaging, robotic-assisted platforms and the emerging integration of artificial intelligence (AI). This narrative review traces the evolution from conventional laparoscopy to fluorescence-guided, robotic-assisted and AI-supported emergency surgery. Publications were identified in PubMed, EMBASE, and Web of Science, prioritizing guidelines, consensus statements, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and recent clinical studies. Current evidence supports a laparoscopic-first approach in hemodynamically stable patients with acute appendicitis, acute calculous cholecystitis, perforated peptic ulcer and selected cases of adhesive small bowel obstruction, strangulated hernia, complicated diverticulitis and penetrating trauma, whereas open laparotomy remains mandatory in hemorrhagic or septic shock. Near-infrared ICG imaging has expanded from elective to emergency practice for biliary anatomy identification, bowel perfusion assessment and anastomotic decision-making, although prospective emergency data remain limited. Robotic systems offer dexterity, ergonomics and integrated imaging, but emergency use is constrained by availability, cost, docking time and team training. AI is being explored for anatomy recognition, risk prediction, augmented reality and autonomous subtasks, with decision support the most realistic near-term role. The value of these technologies depends less on the technology itself than on patient selection, surgical expertise, team preparedness and institutional readiness.
