### 14. [A rapid detection model and device for pH value in water quality based on ratio fluorescence imaging](https://doi.org/10.2166/wst.2026.364)

**Authors:** Min Pu, Tingting Zhang, Guangqi Liu, Tai Ma, Minshou Wang, Ming Sun  
**Venue/category:** Water Science & Technology  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.2166/wst.2026.364`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 15  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging, fluorescent probe  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Summary: Met-GQDs and GSH-AuNCs form a ratiometric fluorescent probe (pH 2-12); a smartphone builds a 2,000-image dataset from 11 pH levels; YOLOv5n and CatBoost give 99.26% pH accuracy; a 3D-printed device and app read pH in 10 s (MAE 0.0440). pH value is a critical physicochemical parameter in water quality assessment. Deviations from the optimal pH range in aquatic environments such as rivers, lakes, and oceans can exert profound and lasting adverse effects on ecosystem stability and human health. In this study, a pH detection system integrating a ratiometric fluorescence imaging strategy was developed. A ratiometric fluorescent probe based on graphene quantum dots and gold nanoclusters was first constructed. Fluorescence imaging data were subsequently captured using a smartphone camera, followed by data augmentation and expansion to establish a robust ratiometric fluorescence image dataset. A hybrid pH detection algorithm combining YOLOv5n-based object detection and CatBoost regression was proposed and implemented. Furthermore, a portable device for on-site water pH measurement was assembled. The proposed model achieved a detection accuracy of 99.26%, demonstrating excellent performance. The practical applicability of the device was validated through pH measurements of real water samples, with comparative analyses against conventional methods confirming its superior detection capability. Overall, the developed pH sensor offers distinct advantages in terms of portability and cost-effectiveness, rendering it suitable for rapid, on-site pH monitoring in diverse water bodies.

### 21. [Second-generation tau PET tracer in situ binding assessed with fluorescence imaging](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11153643/v1)

**Authors:** Aleksandra Antic, Ambra Dreos, Tammaryn Lashley, Stephen Yong Yo-Ap, Kerstin Sander, Michael Schöll, Jörg Hanrieder  
**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**DOI:** `10.21203/rs.3.rs-11153643/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 9  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging  

**Abstract**

Abstract Brain positron emission tomography (PET) imaging is a valuable tool which allows visualization of the pathological structures inside the living patients’ brains. While amyloid-β PET tracers have been in use with Alzheimer’s disease patients in clinics, tau PET tracers have still not entered the clinical routine. Second-generation tau PET tracers have been developed with the aim to overcome the limitations of the first-generation tau PET tracers, such as low sensitivity and off-target binding. However, the detailed pathological characterization of these tracers’ binding is still needed for fully understanding their potential as accurate markers of pathogenic tau. Herein, we focused on the second-generation tau PET tracer Genentech Tau Probe 1 ([ 18 F]GTP1) and made use of the fluorescent properties of its non-radioactive unlabeled variant GTP1 for correlative fluorescence microscopy. The results show that GTP1 binds to tau tangles and neuropil threads (NPTs), but not amyloid-β plaques. Importantly, our results indicate that there is a strong correlation between the fluorescent GTP1 signal and the [ 18 F]GTP1 signal detected with autoradiography, which is currently the standard way of studying the tau PET tracer binding in situ . Together, this data shows the robust application of a non-radioactive tau PET tracer in binding studies in post-mortem tissue and sheds new light on the tau binding properties of GTP1.

### 24. [Environmentally Sustainable Chitin-Based Composite Structures Isolated from Lichens: Physicochemical Characterization and Biointerface Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.17776/csj.1824406)

**Authors:** Zekiye Kocakaya, Behlul Koc-Bilican, Fatma Kılıç Dokan, Mustafa Kocakaya, Gökçe Şeker Karatoprak, Selen İlgün, Fatih Duman  
**Venue/category:** Cumhuriyet Science Journal  
**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**DOI:** `10.17776/csj.1824406`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** biointerface  

**Abstract**

Lichens are complex symbiotic organisms composed of fungi and photosynthetic partners such as algae or cyanobacteria. While numerous bioactive compounds have been extracted from lichens, their insoluble, structural polymeric materials remain largely unexplored. In this study, the structural components of four lichen species—Xanthoparmelia pulla, Rhizoplaca chrysoleuca, Diploschistes scruposus, and Umbilicaria nylanderiana—were isolated through sequential demineralization, deproteinization, and decolorization processes to obtain sustainable biopolymeric matrices. The isolated materials were characterized using FTIR, XRD, TGA, SEM, and EDS analyses. The combined physicochemical analyses supported the presence of chitin-containing structural polysaccharides together with residual mineral components and revealed species-dependent differences in the chemical, crystalline, thermal, morphological, and elemental characteristics of the isolated materials. XRD analysis further revealed distinct diffraction profiles and contributions from residual crystalline mineral phases, particularly CaCO₃. SEM observations indicated species-dependent differences in surface morphology, while EDS confirmed the presence of C, N, O, Si, and Ca elements. The MTT assay revealed species-, concentration-, and time-dependent differences in L929 cell viability. Materials derived from X. pulla and R. chrysoleuca showed comparatively favorable cellular responses, whereas D. scruposus and U. nylanderiana caused significant reductions in cell viability. These findings provide comparative physicochemical and cellular-response data on lichen-derived structural materials and support their further investigation as naturally derived materials for potential biointerface applications.

### 25. [Cybertissue neuroprosthetics: a new implantable neuroprosthetics paradigm to achieve seamless lifetime integration](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/aeb442)

**Authors:** Liang Guo  
**Venue/category:** Journal of Neural Engineering  
**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**DOI:** `10.1088/1741-2552/aeb442`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** neural interface  

**Abstract**

Abstract Objective. Despite decades of advances in neural interface technology, the long-term instability of implantable neuroprostheses remains a major barrier to achieving reliable lifetime performance. Chronic foreign-body reactions and fibrotic encapsulation progressively degrade the electrode-tissue interface, leading to signal deterioration and eventual device failure. Existing strategies that focus on improving material biocompatibility, reducing mechanical mismatch, or modifying implant surfaces have produced important but largely incremental improvements, while leaving the fundamental challenge unresolved. This perspective paper introduces a fundamentally different paradigm: cybertissue neuroprosthetics (CN). Approach. CN moves beyond the traditional electrode‐based neural interface by treating the implant itself as a functional tissue device—composed of living cells and optionally augmented with minimal nanoelectronics, nanomaterials, or genetic modifications—designed to integrate structurally and functionally with the host nervous system to achieve long‐term neuromodulation and information exchange. By minimizing or eliminating foreign materials and leveraging the brain’s own cellular signaling mechanisms, CN promises to overcome the inherent limitations of current inorganic neuroprosthetic devices and seeks to achieve seamless lifetime integration between neuroprosthetic devices and the nervous system. Main results. This paper introduces the CN concept, unifies a range of emerging research directions under this umbrella, and articulates the core opportunities—from engineered cybercells to transplantable living devices—that collectively define a new interdisciplinary frontier. Significance. We argue that CN represents a foundational shift in neuroprosthetics, with the potential to deliver truly lifetime neural implants that are biologically integrated, self‐sustaining, and clinically transformative.
