### 2. [Organic Fluorophores for Single-Molecule Fluorescence Imaging: Strategies, Challenges, and Perspectives](https://doi.org/10.1021/cbmi.6c00170)

**Authors:** Bingjie Zhao, Quan Qi, Guoqing Xiao, Yuhao Chang, André Jung, Huiqin Wang, Tingting Gong, Chaochao Fan, Zhi Wang, Heqi Gao, Jen-Shyang Ni, Dongfeng Dang  
**Venue/category:** Chemical & Biomedical Imaging  
**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**DOI:** `10.1021/cbmi.6c00170`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 18  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging, organic fluorophore  

**Abstract**

Abstract Single-molecule fluorescence techniques have been listed as the important tools in biophysics, chemical biology and biomedical research, enabling the resolution of molecular heterogeneity, transient states, and nanoscale biological organization that are hidden in ensemble measurements. In these experiments, organic small-molecule fluorophores are widely used as labels for single-molecule Förster resonance energy transfer (smFRET), single-molecule tracking, single-molecule localization microscopy (SMLM), and stimulated emission depletion (STED) microscopy. Their performance, however, is limited by a finite photon budget, blinking, photobleaching, and the local environment. This review discusses recent strategies to improve the photostability and emission stability of organic fluorophores under the single-molecule conditions, including dye scaffold and substituent engineering, self-healing dyes, oxygen-scavenging systems, redox buffer systems, photostabilizing additives, microenvironmental regulation, and illumination optimization. Building on this framework, this review further examines the applicability limits of these strategies in complex biological systems, as well as remaining challenges in live-cell compatibility, near-infrared (NIR) fluorophore design, standardized performance evaluation, and data-driven optimization of experimental conditions.

### 12. [Highly Sensitive Piezoresistance Conductive Fabric Based on Enhanced PEDOT:PSS/MWCNTs for Electronic Wearable Devices](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaelm.6c01835)

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 12  
**Matched:** pedot:pss, electronic skin  

**Abstract**

Abstract Conductive fabrics are promising for applications in smart textiles and wearable electronics because they are lightweight, flexible, and easy to integrate. However, constructing a continuous, stable, and highly efficient functional organic/inorganic conductive network within the porous fibrous architecture remains a major challenge for the fabrication of high-performance conductive fabrics. In this study, PEDOT:PSS was modified via a high-speed non-solvent turbulent secondary doping (HNTD) process. Using the modified PEDOT:PSS as the carrier, multiwalled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) were loaded into cotton nonwoven fabrics through a simple dip-coating method to fabricate PEDOT:PSS/MWCNTs composite conductive fabrics (PPMF). Through π–π interactions between PEDOT:PSS and MWCNTs, this approach promoted the uniform distribution of MWCNTs throughout the fabric fibers. It also substantially suppressed the flake-like aggregation of doped PEDOT:PSS during solvent evaporation, thereby improving electron transport within the conductive fabric. The resulting PPMF exhibited excellent electrical performance, with a conductivity of 2.96 × 10–2 S cm–1. The assembled flexible PPMF pressure sensor exhibited ultrahigh sensitivity (108.15 kPa–1) and rapid response and recovery times of 18 and 35 ms, respectively. This performance was attributed to the multilevel interwoven network of the cotton fabric and the uniform, synergistic organic/inorganic conductive network, which enabled the effective detection of minute external forces. Moreover, PPMF can be integrated into wearable sensors to monitor movements at different body sites, including large joint motions and subtle facial movements. This work therefore provides a viable strategy for developing next-generation textile electronics and electronic skin.

**Abstract**

As citrus production expands into northern Florida and neighboring states, where environmental conditions are considered marginal due to increased exposure to freezing events, identifying cold-tolerant genotypes has become a critical priority. This study addresses the urgent need for climate-resilient agriculture by using high-throughput plant phenotyping to evaluate the freezing tolerance of the ‘Marathon’ mandarin (cv. UF 1420) grafted onto 11 diverse citrus rootstocks. Using a controlled stress environment of −4 °C, we integrated chlorophyll fluorescence imaging and kinetics analysis in citrus plants to map the functional and structural stability of the photosynthetic apparatus under stress. Multivariate principal component analysis followed by linear discriminant analysis effectively discriminated tolerance levels, pinpointing four key parameters: electron transport flux per active reaction center (ET 0 /RC), initial slope of the relative variable fluorescence ( M 0 ), absorption flux per active reaction center (ABS/RC), and dissipation flux per active reaction center (DI 0 /RC), as the most powerful physiological indicators of freeze-induced damage. In contrast, rootstocks such as KH, Rich 16-6, UFR-4, and US 897 exhibited significant reaction center inactivation and severe photoinhibition, leading to high mortality rates 6 weeks after stress. This research provides an analytical approach for citrus breeding programs based on the characterization of photosynthetic responses, demonstrating that the stability of photosynthetic processes is a key determinant of cold resilience. By identifying freezing-resilient citrus rootstocks, this work provides actionable insights for selecting rootstocks adapted to North Florida conditions through the characterization of photosynthetic responses, thereby supporting the long-term viability of the regional citrus industry.

**Abstract**

Abstract Silver nanowires (AgNWs) are promising materials for stretchable transparent electrodes (STEs) due to their high aspect ratio, excellent electrical conductivity, and optical transparency. However, the flexibility of AgNW-based STEs is often constrained by the significant modulus mismatch between the rigid AgNWs and soft polymer substrates. Herein, we introduce acrylated polyrotaxane (PRA) between the AgNW network and a polyurethane (PU) substrate, resulting in a highly stretchable and transparent electrode. The pulley effect of the acrylated polyrotaxane helps alleviate stress concentration at the AgNW–PU interface. Therefore, the network structure of AgNWs can remain relatively intact even under large tensile strains. The resulting composite electrode combines a transmittance of about 86.8%, a sheet resistance of 47 Ω·sq–1, and a stretchability of up to 130%. The as-prepared AgNWs/PRA/PU electrode demonstrates great potential for applications in stretchable electroluminescent devices and electrophysiological signal monitoring. This study offers a straightforward and effective strategy for developing high-performance stretchable electrodes, presenting a viable pathway for next-generation electronic devices.

### 27. [How Much of the Invisible Can Become Visible: Indocyanine Green Fluorescence Cholangiography During Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy—A Review on Biliary Anatomy Visualization, Protocol Heterogeneity and Operative Outcomes](https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina62112098)

**Authors:** Cătălin Aliuș, Dragoș Șerban, Vlad Alexandru Gâta, Radu Alexandru Ilieș, Gerald Gheorghe Filip, Ciprian Antoniu Horațiu Tănăsescu, Laurențiu Simion, Cătălin Cicerone Grigorescu, Irina Shevchenko, Bogdan Mihai Cristea, Mihai-Ștefan Mureșan, George Călin Dindelegan et al.

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** near-infrared fluorescence  

**Abstract**

Background and Objectives: In difficult clinical settings, laparoscopic cholecystectomy remains linked to higher conversion rates, prolonged operative time and an increased risk of bile duct injury. Near-infrared fluorescence cholangiography using indocyanine green (ICG) was developed as a real-time intraoperative imaging technique aimed at improving biliary anatomy visualization, reducing the need for cystic duct cannulation or use of ionizing radiation. We hypothesize that the variable performance of ICG fluorescence cholangiography might not be an intrinsic property of the technique, depending instead on the administration protocol, the specifications of the NIR devices and technical factors, patient characteristics, and the severity of the disease. The aim of the present narrative review with systematic search is to critically evaluate the available primary clinical evidence on the role of ICG fluorescence cholangiography as a tool in general and in difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy, with special focus on biliary anatomy visualization, protocol heterogeneity, operative outcomes and evidence limitations. Materials and Methods: We conducted a systematic literature search in PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science to identify original studies published between 2015–2026 that evaluated ICG fluorescence during laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Afterwards, findings were synthesized narratively based on clinically relevant themes. Results: A total of 24 studies were included in the analysis. Some studies reported shorter times to biliary structure identification and achievement of the critical view of safety. Considerable heterogeneity persists regarding several variables: ICG dose and timing, the route of administration, and patient selection. Conclusions: Globally, the main benefits of ICG fluorescence cholangiography are the enhancement of biliary anatomy identification and the improvement of operative efficiency. Future randomized trials using standardized fluorescence protocols are needed to determine the optimal administration routes and doses of ICG and identify whether improved visualization translates into meaningful surgical and clinical improvements.
