### 7. [Reprogramming neural repair through the convergence of gene editing and bioelectronic scaffolds](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1984052)

**Authors:** Ying Li, Kaizhi Cai, Lei Zhao, Yu Liu, Guowang Li, Wenli Xing, Ming Yu  
**Venue/category:** Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology  
**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**DOI:** `10.3389/fbioe.2026.1984052`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 13  
**Matched:** bioelectronic, electrophysiological  

**Abstract**

Neural repair research increasingly combines molecular interventions with engineered environments that support axonal growth and circuit function. This narrative review examines neuronal growth pathways, direct cellular reprogramming, conductive scaffolds, extracellular-vesicle delivery, and neuroimmune and neurovascular interactions. Genetic studies of PTEN/mTOR, SOCS3/STAT3, and growth-associated transcription factors provide a biological foundation for investigating targeted gene regulation. CRISPR-based editing and transcriptional control offer distinct tools for exploring these pathways. Conductive and stimulus-responsive materials provide experimental interfaces for structural guidance, electrical stimulation, and local molecular delivery. Their integration with gene-regulatory systems is presented as a conceptual strategy for coordinating molecular and biophysical interventions. Organoids, single-cell and spatial profiling, and electrophysiological measurements offer complementary approaches to characterizing cell identity and network responses. Clinical studies targeting axon-growth inhibition provide a separate translational evidence stream, with outcomes interpreted according to intervention, injury characteristics, and trial design. Priorities for integrated repair systems include cell-specific delivery, material stability, durable circuit function, and longitudinal safety assessment. A prospective digital-twin workflow is proposed to connect biological measurements with computational predictions and experimentally evaluated intervention choices. Together, these research directions support a framework for studying programmable neural repair across molecular, cellular, and systems levels.

**DOI:** `10.3389/fmicb.2026.1948704`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 12  
**Matched:** bioimaging  

**Abstract**

Blood banks represent highly controlled healthcare environments where microbiological surveillance has traditionally focused on Blood products rather than environmental microbial reservoirs. Despite their crucial role in transfusion safety, the ecology of surface-associated microorganisms remains poorly understood. This cross-sectional study characterized culturable bacteria recovered from Blood-bank surfaces. We combined scanning electron microscopy, culture-based microbiology, phenotypic characterization, MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry, and whole-genome sequencing to investigate whether surfaces within a public blood bank facility (BBF) constitute reservoirs of environmentally derived bacteria with putative traits potentially relevant to environmental survival. Samples collected from a public BBF in Tucumán, Argentina yielded 36 culturable bacterial isolates, predominantly Gram-positive taxa together with a limited number of opportunistic Gram-negative species. Several isolates showed reduced susceptibility to various groups of antimicrobials; furthermore, several strains exhibited strong biofilm formation, production of amyloid-like fibers, motility, and hemolytic activity. Whole-genome sequencing of six representative isolates supported previous species affiliations, identified genes related to antimicrobial resistance, adhesion, biofilm formation, stress adaptation, and cytotoxicity, highlighting the importance of integrating genomic and phenotypic analyses. Notably, BS-018 showed a low ANI to the reported comparison genome, but its taxonomic assignment remains unresolved. Blood bank surfaces function as localized environmental conditions favoring bacteria with potential persistence-associated traits. This study identified environmental bacterial isolates exhibiting phenotypic and genomic characteristics potentially relevant to biosafety surveillance. Our results support incorporating integrated bioimaging, phenotypic characterization, and environmental surveillance with genomic resolution into infection prevention strategies and transfusion biosafety programs.

### 13. [Near-Infrared Emitting Cu(I) Cluster-Based Metal–Organic Frameworks with Thermally Activated Delayed Fluorescence](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.inorgchem.6c04743)

**Authors:** Beibei Wang, Yu Wu, Hai-Yan Li, David James Young, Zhi-Gang Ren, Shuang Chen, Hong-Xi Li  
**Venue/category:** Inorganic Chemistry  
**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acs.inorgchem.6c04743`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence  

**Abstract**

Abstract Reaction of 4-methylpyrimidine-2-thione (Hmpymt) with excess CuCl or CuCN at 150 °C gave two [Cu6(mpymt)6] cluster-based metal–organic frameworks {(CuCl)4[Cu6(mpymt)6]}n (1) and {(CuCN)4[Cu6(mpymt)6]·2Me2CO}n (2). The solid-state structures of 1 and 2 consisted of three-dimensional frameworks in which quasi-hexagonal [Cu6(mpymt)6] units are connected by [Cu4Cl4] units in 1 and [CuCN]n chains in 2. Compounds 1 and 2 exhibited strong near-infrared emission (λem = 737 and 744 nm) with photoluminescence quantum yields of 18.4% and 25.2% at ambient temperature, respectively. Fitting of the temperature-dependent lifetimes yielded model-derived singlet–triplet energy gap estimates of 0.060 and 0.077 eV for 1 and 2, respectively, with model-derived thermally activated delayed fluorescence contributions of approximately 72% and 66% at 300 K. The corresponding room-temperature emission lifetimes were 3.60 and 4.85 μs. A near-infrared phosphor-converted light-emitting diode device fabricated from 2 demonstrated promise for night-vision and potential near-infrared imaging applications.

### 14. [An Embedded Plasticization Strategy for Flexible Thermally Activated Delayed Fluorescence Polymers with High Luminance](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c15384)

**Venue/category:** Journal of the American Chemical Society  
**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**DOI:** `10.1021/jacs.6c15384`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence  

**Abstract**

Abstract Thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) polymers offer the theoretical advantage of achieving 100% internal quantum efficiency. Together with their solution processability, TADF polymers are promising candidates for flexible display technologies. However, emissive polymers that simultaneously combine efficient TADF emission with mechanical flexibility remain rare. Here, we report an embedded plasticized linker strategy for mechanically compliant TADF polymers. Polyethylene glycol (PEG)-based flexible segments are covalently incorporated into acridine–benzophenone–carbazole polymer backbones. This “TADF unit + plasticized linker” design preserves the donor–acceptor electronic structure required for delayed fluorescence while introducing flexibility into the emissive polymer framework. The resulting polymers exhibit small singlet–triplet energy gaps, clear delayed fluorescence, and favorable film deformability. The deformation behavior of the neat polymer films was evaluated on PDMS substrates under an applied substrate strain of 75%. Notably, the optimized rigid organic light-emitting diode (OLED) based on PIDC(2,7)-EO achieves a maximum external quantum efficiency (EQE) of 20.1% and a high luminance of 85 988 cd m–2. This luminance value is among the highest reported for polymer-based TADF OLEDs. Flexible OLEDs fabricated on flexible substrates further deliver a maximum EQE of 14.7%. These results demonstrate efficient electroluminescence from flexible device architectures. This work establishes embedded plasticized linkers as an effective molecular design strategy for high-performance mechanically flexible TADF polymers. This strategy provides a promising route toward next-generation flexible OLED technologies.

### 15. [Off-axis compressed encoding microscopy for high-resolution, high-throughput, high-contrast volumetric imaging](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.ap.9.1.016004)

**Authors:** Dejie Zhang, Tao Jiang, Hui Gong  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Photonics  
**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**DOI:** `10.1117/1.ap.9.1.016004`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** biological imaging  

**Abstract**

High-throughput volumetric fluorescence microscopy requires depth-dependent information to be preserved while maintaining lateral resolution, optical-sectioning contrast, and acquisition speed. Here, we introduce off-axis compressed encoding microscopy (OCEM), a volumetric imaging method that encodes depth-dependent information directly into accelerated two-dimensional measurements. OCEM achieves physics-defined depth encoding: the illumination profile and off-axis multiline detection geometry generate detector-row-specific intensity fingerprints that vary with axial position. These fingerprints provide depth constraints directly in the acquired measurements, enabling a lightweight decoder to recover depth-resolved volumes with optical-sectioning contrast. OCEM combines 4× axial compression with 4× lateral acceleration, achieving a 16-fold increase in volumetric acquisition throughput based on camera exposure time. Because the encoding is implemented within the existing off-axis detection geometry, OCEM requires no additional beam splitters, cameras, or detection paths. We validate OCEM in whole-brain imaging and volumetric FISH, demonstrating preserved structural continuity, optical-sectioning contrast, and signal-localization consistency for large-scale quantitative biological imaging.

**DOI:** `10.1002/slct.74866`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** fluorescent probe  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT The similar chemical structures and activities of ROS pose significant challenges for selective detection. Small molecule fluorescent probes based on the principle of intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) have become important tools for ROS detection due to their high sensitivity, spatial and temporal resolution, and real time imaging capabilities. In comparison to solvent‐sensitive excited‐state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT), intensity‐responsive photoinduced electron transfer (PET), and donor (D)–acceptor (A) distance‐controlled Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET), ICT exhibits superior performance due to significant changes in absorption and emission spectra as well as fluorescence quantum yield under laser radiation. However, there is currently a lack of systematic review of ICT‐based fluorescent probes for ROS imaging. This review systematically summarizes organic small‐molecule fluorescent probes based on the ICT mechanism and published between 2020 and 2026, focusing on their design strategies, application in the detection of ROS, including HClO, ONOO − , and H 2 O 2 . We believe that this review will provide profound insights and guidance for the preparation of new ICT ROS fluorescent probe.

### 17. [Compressed sensing-enabled super-resolution fluorescence microscopy: a comprehensive review](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.ap.9.1.014001)

**Authors:** Yunhua Yao, Dalong Qi, Yuecheng Shen, Hongmei Ma, Zhenrong Sun, Shian Zhang  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Photonics  
**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**DOI:** `10.1117/1.ap.9.1.014001`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** biological imaging  

**Abstract**

By overcoming the diffraction limit of light, super-resolution fluorescence microscopy has become an indispensable tool for visualizing subcellular structures and dynamic biological processes. Nevertheless, conventional super-resolution techniques generally require extensive spatial or temporal sampling, resulting in prolonged acquisition times, increased photobleaching and phototoxicity, and limited imaging throughput. Compressed sensing, which exploits the inherent sparsity to recover rich information from undersampled measurements, provides an effective computational framework for addressing these limitations. It has significantly improved imaging speed, acquisition efficiency, and overall system performance, thereby enabling new opportunities for observing dynamic biological events. In this review, we present a comprehensive overview of the fundamental principles, methodological developments, and representative applications of compressed sensing-enabled super-resolution fluorescence microscopy. We further compare the strengths and limitations of different strategies, highlight the remaining challenges, and discuss emerging research directions and future opportunities. These techniques are expected to further expand the capabilities of super-resolution microscopy and play an increasingly important role in high-speed, high-throughput, and low-phototoxicity biological imaging.
