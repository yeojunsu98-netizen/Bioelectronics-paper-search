### 1. [Body-Coupled Organic Electrochemical Transistors for Imperceptible Skin Bioelectronics](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c14607)

**Authors:** Cong Li, Shujing Zhong, Jianyi Deng, Xudong Tao, Shenyi Huang, Yueran Qiu, Wenyu Wang, Kaishun Wu  
**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsami.6c14607`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 72  
**Matched:** bioelectronics, bioelectronic, biointerface, organic electrochemical transistor, organic electrochemical transistors, oect, electrochemical transistor  

**Abstract**

Abstract The efficient ion-to-electron transduction mechanism makes organic electrochemical transistors (OECTs) stand out for physiological sensing. Usually, OECT operations require saline solutions or hydrogels to function as the electrolytes; however, the wet electrolytes may compromise wear comfort and device stability for human-integrated applications. In this work, we propose a body-coupled OECT (b-OECT) device mechanism, where the skin and subcutaneous tissues act as natural electrolyte for the device operation. In the b-OECT, the organic semiconducting channel forms intimate and substrate-free contact with skins, enabling biofluid-derived ionic pathways to directly mediate gate–channel coupling and electrochemical modulation. The devices exhibit body-coupled transistor characteristics, skin-coupled operation, and ion-mediated neuromorphic-like conductance modulation. Without needing external supplied electrolytes, the b-OECT offers breathable and imperceptible body integration, where biological tissue directly participates in electrochemical signal transduction, opening potentials for skin-integrated bioelectronics and body-coupled neuromorphic biointerfaces.

### 2. [Differential sensing with imprinted and non-imprinted organic electrochemical transistors reduces calibration drift in wearable sweat cortisol sensors](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.31.763347)

**Authors:** Lama I. Almofeez, Mohammad Shafiqul Islam, Thilina S. Ambagahawaththa, Yasser Khan  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.64898/2026.10.31.763347`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 45  
**Matched:** organic electrochemical transistor, organic electrochemical transistors, pedot:pss, electrochemical transistor  

**Abstract**

The output of a wearable cortisol sensor drifts over days for reasons unrelated to cortisol, so a calibration made on the first day soon stops being valid. In our differential organic electrochemical transistor, a PEDOT:PSS transistor coated with a molecularly imprinted polymer (MIP) for cortisol is paired with one coated with a non-imprinted polymer (NIP) of the same composition, and the NIP response is subtracted. Over four calibrations in artificial sweat across 14 days, the response of the MIP alone shifted by 15% to 22% of its range on day 1, whereas the differential output shifted by 1% to 10%, and the variation between days at 1 to 10nM fell by 65% to 70% on average for individual pairs. In sweat from one participant, read against the calibration from day 1, both outputs tracked the direction of visible changes in salivary cortisol, while the MIP alone diverged from the differential output, consistent with the laboratory drift.

### 6. [Mitochondria-Localized Three-Color Fluorescent Sensor for Concurrent Tracking of Mitochondrial H2O2, Hypochlorite and Subcellular Viscosity during Oxidative Stress and Drug-Related Hepatic Damage](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.analchem.6c06114)

**Authors:** Xufeng Hou, Zhen Yang, Jiahui Li, Mengchao Guo, Zhihong Xu, Song Chen, Mingming Yu  
**Venue/category:** Analytical Chemistry  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acs.analchem.6c06114`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 12  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging, fluorescent probe  

**Abstract**

Abstract We report a naphthalimide derived mitochondrial targeting fluorescent probe MT for real time simultaneous visualization of mitochondrial hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), hypochlorite (ClO−), and subcellular microviscosity, three key biomarkers closely associated with oxidative stress mediated drug induced liver injury. Free MT shows negligible background fluorescence under physiological conditions, and each biomarker triggers well-separated emission bands: red emission at 680 nm for elevated viscosity, orange emission at 600 nm for H2O2, and green emission at 500 nm for ClO−. MT exhibits low detection limits of 123.24 nM for H2O2 and 6.34 nM for ClO−, enabling spectral crosstalk free triple channel readouts. We validated MT performance in ferroptosis and drug damaged hepatocyte cell models for in situ triple biomarker imaging. In vivo fluorescence imaging dynamically monitored acetaminophen provoked acute liver injury in mice; full three channel signals were only acquired from ex vivo liver slices due to tissue penetration constraints. This sensitive multichannel probe provides a practical tool to dissect liver pathological progressions and quantitatively evaluate hepatoprotective agents in preclinical liver injury models.

### 7. [Injectable Ultrahigh Molecular Weight Fluorescent Copolymers for Bioimaging and Surgical Guidance](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biomac.6c01343)

**Authors:** Yichun Yuan, Sophia Beilharz, Audrey N. Hostetler, Heather R. Everson, Era Srivastava, LeAnne Young, Nehal Nupnar, Oliver B. Giebelhaus, Michael J. A. Hore, Faruk H. Örge, Divita Mathur, Metin Karayilan  
**Venue/category:** Biomacromolecules  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acs.biomac.6c01343`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 12  
**Matched:** bioimaging  

**Abstract**

Abstract Injectable fluorescent polymers can enable real-time surgical guidance and bioimaging, but combining high transparency, low cellular uptake, clinically relevant viscosity, and hydrolytic stability remains challenging. Here, we report ultrahigh molecular weight (UHMW, &gt;106 g mol−1) fluorescent copolymers synthesized by photoiniferter RAFT polymerization as injectable biomaterials for fluorescent ophthalmic viscosurgical devices (OVDs). Simulation results showed that fluorophore-rich segments collapse, promoting aggregation and self-quenching, and identified random monomer distribution with low fluorophore loading as key design criteria. Guided by these insights, N,N-dimethylacrylamide was copolymerized with fluorescein acrylate (0.1−1 mol %) to produce poly(DMA-co-FluA) with molecular weights up to 1.4 MDa and bright green fluorescence under blue illumination. Compared with (meth)acrylate analogues, the acrylamide backbone provided access to UHMW and optical clarity while offering greater intrinsic hydrolytic stability. The 1.4 MDa copolymer matched commercial Healon GV viscosity at ∼30 wt %, exhibited a pronounced frequency-dependent decrease in complex viscosity, reduced HEK293 cellular internalization, and enabled ex vivo porcine-eye OVD visualization and visual assessment of residual material.

### 14. [Osteoarthritis‐Microenvironment‐Triggered Phase Alteration of Injectable Conductive Hydrogel for Attenuating Cartilage Degradation](https://doi.org/10.1002/adhm.71943)

**Authors:** Sunu Hangma Subba, Kaustuv Roy, Ee Hyun Kim, Yo Bin Hong, Eun‐Jung Jin, Akhmad Irhas Robby, Sung Young Park  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Healthcare Materials  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.1002/adhm.71943`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** conductive hydrogel  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Osteoarthritis (OA) is associated with excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, which contributes substantially to chondrocyte dysfunction, oxidative stress, and cartilage degradation. Although stimuli‐responsive hydrogels have emerged as promising platforms for OA therapy, conventional hydrogel‐based systems exhibit limited sensitivity and require external detection equipment, highlighting the need for a simple, sensitive, and directly observable monitoring system. To address these challenges, we developed a ROS‐triggered, in situ‐forming gel‐sol transition of a polydopamine (PDA)‐loaded, diselenide‐crosslinked polymer dot (PD) injectable hydrogel (HGC‐PDA@PD or OA gel) as a monitorable theranostic for OA therapy at physiological temperature (37°C). The monitorable OA microenvironment‐responsive hydrogel relied on interactions between the high ROS levels in OA cartilage and the OA gel, which triggered diselenide bond cleavage and the release of PDA from PD, causing a unique gel‐sol transition of the OA gel. Owing to PDA release after diselenide breakage, the fluorescence and electroconductivity of the OA gel changed, which could serve as additional signals during OA detection. In vitro studies demonstrated the sensitive gel‐sol transition of OA gel towards OA‐induced immature articular chondrocytes (IL‐1β/siSOD1/siCatalase‐transfected iMACs). Furthermore, the OA gel showed a distinct gel‐sol transition in cartilage of an in vivo OA mouse model compared with that in control mice. The lower transcription levels of cartilage matrix‐degrading enzymes (ADAMTS4, ADAMTS5, MMP‐3, and MMP‐13) observed after OA gel injection confirmed that the OA gel could scavenge ROS and attenuate cartilage damage, revealing its potential application for dual‐function OA diagnosis and rehabilitation.

### 15. [Mechanistic modeling of exciton–exciton annihilation in the prototypical thermally activated delayed fluorescence emitter DMAC-TRZ](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0352742)

**Authors:** Christian B. McDonald, Clint van Hoesel, Peter A. Bobbert, Reinder Coehoorn  
**Venue/category:** Journal of Applied Physics  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.1063/5.0352742`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence  

**Abstract**

Organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) that are based on thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) emitter molecules can potentially outcompete often-used OLEDs that are based on metalorganic phosphorescent emitters, as due to a larger radiative decay rate of TADF emitters, the efficiency loss at high current densities (“roll off”) due to bimolecular interaction processes can be reduced. However, so far no mechanistic model of these processes has been developed. Here, we develop such a model for exciton–exciton annihilation processes in the prototypical TADF emitter 10-(4-(4,6-diphenyl-1,3,5-triazin-2-yl)phenyl)-9,9-dimethyl-9,10-dihydroacridine. Using kinetic Monte Carlo simulations to analyze the results of time-resolved photoluminescence experiments, singlet–singlet and singlet–triplet annihilation are shown to be well described as a Förster-type process with a Förster radius of 3.8 nm. This finding is shown to be consistent with results of first-principles quantum-chemical calculations. Triplet–triplet annihilation is found to be negligible under optical excitation.

### 16. [Electrospinning Versus Chemical Polymerization for Conductive Polymer Fibers: Reproducibility, Electrical Performance and Wearable Sensing](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym18212730)

**Authors:** Abdullah Bin Bashir, Dilip Depan  
**Venue/category:** Polymers  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.3390/polym18212730`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** conductive polymer  

**Abstract**

Conductive polymers (CPs) such as polyaniline (PANI), polypyrrole (PPy) and poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) (PEDOT) combine electronic function with the processability of plastics, and one-dimensional CP fibers add high surface area and directional charge transport. This review compares chemical oxidative polymerization and electrospinning against three criteria: how fiber geometry is reported and controlled, electrical performance, and wearable-sensor behavior. The comparison separates the chemistry that forms the polymer from the operation that forms the fiber, because the two are frequently conflated. Fiber diameter is reported as a numeric range wherever a dedicated spinning step defines it, whether by electrospinning or by wet spinning, and qualitatively where the fiber forms by precipitation during polymerization. This is a difference in reporting practice and in dimensional uniformity; it is not evidence of batch to batch or inter-laboratory reproducibility, for which no data were found for either route. Electrical comparison is complicated by measurement geometry, since single-fiber, mat, pellet and sheet-resistance values span one to three orders of magnitude across the surveyed literature and are not interchangeable. Where a single study varies one factor at a time, carrier removal and post-spinning conversion recover much of the apparent electrical deficit of electrospun fibers. Reported wearable-sensing metrics are not comparable across transduction modes, and the surveyed devices differ in composition and added conductive phases, so no ranking by fabrication route is drawn. The two routes are complementary: chemical polymerization supplies the conducting phase and scales by batch volume, while electrospinning sets the fiber architecture.

### 17. [Biocompatible fluorescein-based temperature sensor for applications in endoscopic surgery](https://doi.org/10.1088/2050-6120/aeb69e)

**Authors:** Afraa Hasan, Gleb S Budylin, Denis A Davydov, Vadim V Zefirov, Evgeny A Shirshin  
**Venue/category:** Methods and Applications in Fluorescence  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.1088/2050-6120/aeb69e`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** fluorescent probe  

**Abstract**

Abstract Temperature monitoring is critical for endoscopic laser surgery to prevent tissue damage. Thermal injuries of the urothelium have been reported as a known complication of laser lithotripsy, however, in most cases real time temperature measurement is not available due to the absence of sensors in conventional endoscopes. In this study, we present an all-optical fluorescein-based thermal sensing approach enabling temperature measurement via an optical fiber, independent of the endoscope model, using a biocompatible fluorescent probe. The temperature-dependent fluorescence response originates from the redistribution between the monoanionic and dianionic forms of fluorescein, which is influenced by albumin through different mechanisms depending on the solution environment. A comparative analysis in water and in saline solution, which is commonly used for irrigation during surgery, revealed distinct environmental contributions to the temperature response of fluorescein emission. In water, a stable fluorescein–albumin complex was formed, accompanied by a systematic shift in the monoanion–dianion equilibrium, leading to strong temperature-dependent spectral changes. In saline solution, which is more relevant to intraoperative irrigation, no stable complex was observed, as shown by time-resolved fluorescence techniques; nevertheless, a reproducible temperature response was retained and was attributed to weaker, non-specific microenvironmental effects, including local pH variations near the protein surface. These findings demonstrate a reproducible temperature-dependent fluorescence response under defined solution conditions and provide a proof of principle for further development of optical temperature-monitoring approaches relevant to minimally invasive procedures.
