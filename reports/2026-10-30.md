**DOI:** `10.3390/s26216877`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 21  
**Matched:** bioelectronic, bioelectronic interface  

**Abstract**

Material-level sensitivity or conductivity alone does not determine whether a wearable sensor produces trustworthy data. The signal must survive body coupling, packaging, wireless transfer, power constraints, and algorithmic interpretation. This cross-layer critical review examines six connected evidence streams: functional nanomaterial platforms, epidermal and implantable bioelectronic interfaces, wireless readout and power, integrated wearable platforms, artificial intelligence (AI)-based interpretation, and human-centred applications. Hysteresis and drift, interface instability, motion and environmental exposure, readout and power limits, placement and calibration dependence, and inter-user variability can degrade information across these layers. Robustness testing and claim-matched validation are needed to establish what the resulting outputs support. We therefore compare technologies through the failure modes and evidence requirements that persist after integration, rather than through isolated peak metrics. The synthesis offers conditional material-selection criteria for specific device roles, distinguishes demonstrated component functions from application-level claims, and identifies the reporting conditions and use-dependent validation priorities needed to interpret those claims.

**DOI:** `10.1242/bio.062873`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 19  
**Matched:** fluorescence lifetime imaging, flim  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT The chrysophyte Hydrurus foetidus belongs to the psychrophilic, rheophilic algae of cold, fast-flowing glacial and alpine streams but remains poorly characterised at the cellular level. We present a systematic optical microscopy study of field-collected material of this organism, combining confocal imaging, quantitative morphometry, three-dimensional volumetry and fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM). Confocal imaging revealed a pseudoparenchymatous architecture: elongated, longitudinally aligned cells dominate the central axis, whereas round cells occupy the peripheral branches, and the two populations differ in volume. High-magnification imaging showed a consistent apical bias in nuclear position along the cell files of the central axis. FLIM of endogenous autofluorescence yielded reproducible lifetime signatures across the green, orange and far-red emission channels; in the far-red emission channel, the chlorophyll a signal resolved into a two-component mixture, and the round-cell population was bimodal in lifetime whereas the elongated population was not. All material derives from a single winter collection; the whole-thallus spatial analyses come from one intact thallus, while morphometry, volumetry and FLIM sampled cells from multiple thalli of that collection, and the data are analysed accordingly. Together, these data provide a morphological and photophysical foundation, and a label-free autofluorescence reference, for future cell-biological studies of H. foetidus and of multicellular organisation among the chrysophytes.

**Abstract**

Abstract Great efforts are currently devoted to the development of graphene Solution-Gated Field-effect transistors (gSGFET) as sensors for biosensing applications and particularly, neural implants. Indeed, with the high carrier mobility of graphene, graphene-based sensors exhibit very high sensitivity and the easy integration of graphene in flexible devices is a clear advantage for implantable technologies by reducing rejection from biological tissues. However, despite the great performances already exhibited by gSGFETs for biosensing, this technology has not shown its full potential yet. This is due to the fact that since the graphene channel is in direct contact with the liquid medium, the carrier mobility, and consequently the sensitivity of a gSGFET, stays below its theoretical value and the sensors performances are subjected to stability issues. Hexagonal Boron Nitride (hBN) has been proved to be a key two-dimensional material that will enable to overcome carrier mobility limitations. Because the access to highly pure large scale-hBN is challenging, we tested here whether more accessible lower-quality commercial Chemical Vapor Deposited Multilayer BN could still be successfully used to improve gSGFETs sensitivity for neural recordings. We demonstrate that even when using a commercial multilayer BN, the performances of gSGFET sensors can be improved to enable neural recording.

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 9  
**Matched:** fluorescence imaging  

**Abstract**

Improved surgical outcomes in patients with pancreatic cancer are largely associated with atraumatic and precise surgical technique. In this context, the most accurate determination of tumor localization, size, and metastases is particularly important. This can be achieved through fluorescence imaging (FI). It allows not only for specifying the localization of tumor foci and mapping lymphatic collectors, but also for a fairly accurate assessment of organ and tissue perfusion. Objective. Systematic review aims to determine the feasibility of using FI in pancreatic surgery (PROSPERO ID: CRD42023475963). Material and methods. The literature search was conducted using the MEDLINE, Embase, and e-Library databases from 31 of October, 2023 y., to 30 of June, 2024 y. This systematic review included all studies describing the use of FI during pancreatic surgery. Results. A total of 383 patients from 32 studies meeting the inclusion criteria were included in the review. The most commonly used fluorescent agent was indocyanine green (ICG), utilized in 28 studies. The authors cite the following important advantages of FI: more accurate determination of tumor and metastatic lesion localization, reliable assessment of organ blood supply and viability before and after resection, and a reduction in the risk of intraoperative injury to the common bile duct during pancreatic surgical interventions. Conclusion. FI in pancreatic surgery has a definite potential for detecting metastatic lesions not identified by radiological imaging methods, providing more precise visualization of tumors and resection margins, reducing the risk of intraoperative complications, and enabling reliable assessment of perfusion and viability of the pancreas, stomach, duodenum, spleen, and liver. This method may also be used to evaluate the effectiveness of neoadjuvant therapy in patients with pancreatic cancer.

**Abstract**

Diabetes mellitus–induced erectile dysfunction is difficult to reverse due to long-term impairment of cavernosal microenvironment. Conventional oral or intracavernosal injection therapies suffer from poor targeting, uneven drug distribution, and potential complications. Here, we propose a soft, wireless electronic patch for treating erectile dysfunction (SWEED) in diabetes mellitus, which enables safe, noninvasive, and controllable modulation of penile microenvironment. **Abstract**

Rapid and accurate analysis of breast tissue is essential for pathological studies and surgical decision support, whereas conventional frozen section pathology relies on time-consuming staining and subjective interpretation, limiting efficiency. This study introduced a multimodal fusion strategy that simultaneously integrates four complementary imaging modalities, including bright-field, phase-contrast, green fluorescence, and blue fluorescence imaging, to comprehensively capture tissue morphology, structural and molecular features. **Abstract**

Abstract Thioglycolic acid (TGA), a common ingredient in hair removal creams and other industrial formulations, owing to its hazardous nature, necessitates sensitive and selective analytical detection. Herein, we report a symmetrical squaraine-based fluorescent probe for the selective detection of TGA, investigated employing fluorescence spectroscopy, NMR, and density functional theory (DFT) calculations. In addition, the probe functions as a molecular logic gate based on its fluorescence response, highlighting its potential for intelligent molecular sensing.
